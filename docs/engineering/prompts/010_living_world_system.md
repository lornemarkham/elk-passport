# Prompt 010 — Living World System

Save as: `docs/engineering/prompts/010_living_world_system.md`

## Purpose

We have over-focused on individual cards.

The product is not a collection of isolated animated widgets. It is one living environment viewed through neighbouring windows.

This prompt defines how cards exist alone, in small groups, and across the full field.

Do not implement until the world laws and execution plan are approved.

## Central Principle

Cards are aware of the world around them.

They should respond to shared environmental forces, nearby presence, group composition, spacing, and occasional events.

The field should feel coordinated without becoming synchronized.

## Three Required Scales

### Individual

Define baseline idle behaviour, personality, energy, breathing rhythm, material response, rare events, pointer response, drag response, recovery, and settling.

### Small Group

Define personal space, attraction and repulsion, alignment without synchronization, shared local wind, light spill, particle travel, curiosity, dominance and quietness, complementary rhythms, hover/drag response, and clutter prevention.

### Full Field

Define global ambient state, shared wind, energy budget, attention budget, rare world events, density management, depth, field-wide settling, anti-synchronization rules, and limits on how many cards may become expressive at once.

## Social and Spatial Properties

Use the smallest useful set of parameters from:

- Preferred personal space
- Confidence
- Curiosity
- Social energy
- Calmness
- Responsiveness
- Influence radius
- Wind sensitivity
- Light sensitivity
- Particle emission
- Attention seeking
- Group compatibility
- Restlessness
- Recovery time

Do not build an over-engineered personality simulation.

## Interactions Between Cards

### Shared Wind

A field-level wind affects fire, smoke, leaves, steam, mist, snow, dust, and pollen differently.

### Light Influence

Campfire glow may warm a nearby card. Water may reflect nearby light. Forest content may catch a passing warm highlight.

### Particle Travel

An ember may leave Campfire, cross shared space, and fade near another card. Released particles must become independent of their source.

### Spatial Negotiation

Cards maintain soft personal space, drift into compatible groupings, and gently separate when crowded.

### Attention Propagation

Hovering one card may cause neighbours to quiet, orient subtly, or create space rather than compete.

### Shared Events

A rare gust may affect several compatible phenomena in coherent but non-identical ways. Birds, insects, shadows, light sweeps, or distant disturbances must remain rare.

## Group Composition

Document rules for pairs, triads, clusters, dense groups, sparse groups, mixed-energy groups, similar personalities, entry/removal, dragging through a group, and group settling.

A high-energy card may contrast with a calm card. Two expressive cards should not peak simultaneously. A hovered card becomes the local protagonist while neighbours support it.

## Attention Budget

At any moment:

- Most cards remain at ambient baseline.
- A small number show elevated life.
- Only one or very few perform a noticeable rare event.
- Hovered or dragged cards receive priority.
- Neighbours support rather than compete.

Define states such as Resting, Ambient, Curious, Supporting, Featured, and Recovering.

## Energy Budget

Define global baseline, local energy, energy transfer, decay, cooldown, drag influence, long-idle behaviour, and reduced-motion behaviour.

## Anti-Patterns

Do not create synchronized bobbing, screensaver magnets, collision chaos, every card reacting to every pointer movement, mechanical orbiting, random helicopter movement, constant viewport-crossing particles, excessive anthropomorphism, obscured controls, nauseating group motion, or a system too complex to tune.

## Required Deliverable Before Code

Produce a permanent specification containing:

1. World principles
2. Individual-card laws
3. Small-group laws
4. Full-field laws
5. Attention budget
6. Energy budget
7. Shared environmental forces
8. Spatial and social parameters
9. Existing-experience examples
10. Anti-patterns
11. Tuning controls
12. Performance constraints
13. Accessibility
14. Phased implementation plan

Wait for approval.
