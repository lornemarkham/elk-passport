# MVP Experience & Visual Contract

## Emotional target

Opening ELK Passport should feel like the beginning of an unforgettable day.

The user should quickly move from:

"I have time off, but I do not know what to do."

to:

"THIS IS GOING TO BE DOPE."

The interface must create anticipation, curiosity, momentum, and confidence. Planning is not administrative work. Planning is the opening act of the adventure.

## Personality

ELK Passport is:

- Bright
- Bold
- Energetic
- Playful
- Slightly rebellious
- Encouraging
- Informative
- Occasionally funny
- Unafraid to be memorable

It may borrow the spirit of ambitious early-2000s interactive websites: dramatic intros, motion, music, video, surprising transitions, and a sense that the website itself is an experience.

Do not reproduce outdated usability problems. Capture the confidence, creativity, spectacle, and fun.

## What it must not become

Do not build:

- A conventional SaaS dashboard
- A sterile itinerary planner
- A generic tourism website
- A form with twenty questions
- A wall of identical cards
- A ChatGPT-style text conversation
- A minimalist beige lifestyle brand
- A purple-gradient "AI app"
- A corporate productivity tool

Clean design is welcome. Lifeless design is not.

## Mobile-first

The primary experience is designed for a phone.

Core actions must be comfortable with one hand, readable outdoors, fast to understand, and usable while moving through an adventure.

Desktop must also feel intentional and impressive, not like a stretched phone layout. Desktop can provide a more cinematic planning and replay experience.

## Visual behaviour

Use strong imagery, confident typography, meaningful motion, layered reveals, and visual rhythm.

The interface should feel alive without becoming confusing.

Motion should:

- Build anticipation
- Reveal plans progressively
- Celebrate meaningful choices
- Create momentum between stages
- Make the final adventure reveal feel important

Motion must not delay routine actions or make the app difficult to use.

## Planning experience

Do not begin with a long questionnaire.

Planning should feel collaborative and visual. Use cards, imagery, reactions, quick choices, sliders or weights where useful, and short conversational prompts.

Whenever confidence is too low to make a strong recommendation, continue learning through enjoyable interactions rather than presenting weak options.

Recommendations should feel like:

"Okay, I think I have something you are going to love."

Not:

"Here are ten nearby attractions."

## Adventure reveal

Do not simply display the completed itinerary.

Reveal the adventure with pacing and personality.

The reveal should make the user excited to begin. It may include strong imagery, short copy, transitions, a suggested soundtrack, a preview of the emotional arc, and moments of surprise.

The user should finish the reveal with a grin and a strong desire to go.

## Adventure mode

Once the adventure begins, the interface becomes simpler and more practical.

It should clearly show:

- What is happening now
- What comes next
- Useful timing and location information
- How to capture a moment
- How to adjust the plan

Adventure Mode should support the experience rather than compete with it.

## Capture and storytelling

Capturing a moment must be extremely lightweight.

Support photos and short notes without turning the adventure into homework.

The completed adventure should become a story worth sharing, not merely a list of completed itinerary items.

The final recap should help the user show friends:

"This is what I did. You should do it too."

A little personality and playful exaggeration are welcome. Not every legendary fish needs photographic evidence.

## NFR

NFR means No Friggin' Regrets for now.

It is both an internal product standard and a feeling users should want to express after completing an adventure.

The exact family-friendly wording can evolve, but the initials NFR must remain.

The experience should end with the user feeling:

- Glad they went
- Proud they used their free time well
- Excited to tell the story
- Ready to plan another adventure

## MVP standard

This is not a disposable wireframe.

It is a focused first version of the real product.

Scope may be small, but the core loop must feel intentional:

Plan → Reveal → Experience → Track → Capture → Recap → Share

The founder must be able to use it tonight to plan tomorrow's real adventure.
