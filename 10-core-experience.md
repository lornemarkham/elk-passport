# Core Experience

Everything in ELK Passport exists to support one capability:

Passport curates a day made up of experiences the user is genuinely delighted to see.

This is the product.

Not AI.

Not maps.

Not itineraries.

Not reviews.

Not search.

Not social features.

Not badges.

Not gamification.

Not travel planning.

Those may become valuable capabilities in the future, but they are not the core experience.

## The core experience

A user opens Passport.

Passport understands who they are.

Passport understands what kind of day they want.

Passport understands their constraints and context.

Passport recommends a flexible day that immediately feels right.

The user's reaction is:

"Yes."

Or:

"Oh... that's exactly what I wanted."

If the recommendation creates delight and confidence, Passport has succeeded.

If the recommendation feels generic, overwhelming, irrelevant, or requires the user to keep searching, Passport has failed.

## The test for every future feature

The purpose of every future feature should be evaluated against one question:

Does this help Passport curate a more delightful day?

If the answer is no, it does not belong in the core experience.

The product is not trying to maximize information.

It is trying to maximize delight, confidence, and anticipation.

The goal is that a user spends less time searching and more time living.

## The smallest successful version

The smallest successful version of ELK Passport is one that can consistently help someone finish the day saying:

"That was a damn good day."
